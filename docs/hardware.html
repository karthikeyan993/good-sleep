Fix or check these

Found while checking datasheets against the plan.

  • Test at step 4
    The green LED wants 4.5–5 V, and the sensor board runs on 3.3 V.Analog Devices recommends 4.5–5 V on the LED supply when using green. SparkFun says the sibling MAX30105 may light green from about 3.5 V. Your plan picked green for the wrist, so test it on the bench before relying on it. If it's weak: use IR at night (you barely move while asleep), and give green a proper 5 V LED supply when you make your own PCB.
  • Fix before wearing
    The sensor board's power LED burns about 1.4 mA, all day and night.That's more than everything else in the band combined, and it halves the battery life. SparkFun's design (which SmartElex copies) has a jumper to cut it. Cut it once the board works.
  • Check before buying
    Some W25Q128 boards use the 1.8 V version of the chip.W25Q128JV runs on 3.3 V; W25Q128JW is a 1.8 V part. Robocraze's 7Semi board is a JW. Only buy a JW board if it has its own regulator and level shifting. Many cheap modules also have an always-on power LED: remove it.
  • Already known
    Raw nights don't fit in the board's 2 MB.About 13–17 MB a night at the planned rates. That's what the optional 16 MB flash chip is for (storage option A in the build plan).
  • Corrected estimate
    The build plan's "2–5 nights per charge" assumed recording 24 hours a day.Recording only at night and sleeping in between, the estimate is closer to 10 nights. The real number comes from measuring at firmware step 11.
  • Firmware rule
    Don't set pin P0.14 high while the battery is charging.Seeed warns it can push P0.31 (the battery-voltage pin) past its 3.6 V limit and damage it.

How it connects

The battery and USB-C feed the XIAO, which charges the battery and makes 3.3 V for everything else. The two external boards hang off its I2C and SPI pins.

Rookie mistakes, and what stops them

The battery's protection circuit already cuts off on overcharge, over-discharge and short circuit, and the XIAO's charger stops when the battery is full. These are the mistakes those don't cover.

  • Kills the board
    Battery connected backwards.Battery connectors have no standard wiring, and the wire colours on cheap cells can be wrong. Check which wire is + with the multimeter every time before connecting, mark it, and solder it directly once you're sure.
  • Sparks, heat
    Battery wires touching.Cut and strip one wire at a time, and tape the other end meanwhile. The protection circuit cuts off a short, but don't test it.
  • Shorts
    Soldering with power connected.Switch the battery off and unplug USB before the iron touches the board. Keep the iron, and any lighter for heat-shrink, away from the battery.
  • Shorts
    A solder bridge between two pins.Before the first power-up, beep-test neighbouring pins with the multimeter. Then power it through a USB power meter: if it draws over about 100 mA while doing nothing, unplug and look. A cheap USB hub between the board and the Mac also shields the Mac's port.
  • Fire risk
    Charging on the bed, or unattended.Charge on a hard surface, inside a LiPo safety bag while it's a prototype, and never while wearing it. Keep the charge rate at 50 mA: gentle for a 150 mAh cell.
  • Stop using
    A puffy battery.Swelling means the cell is damaged. Disconnect it, don't puncture it, and take it to e-waste.
  • Firmware
    Draining the battery flat.A bug that leaves the sensors on can run it down to the protection cut-off. Have the firmware stop recording below about 3.5 V, and keep the P0.14 rule.
  • Small stuff
    Eyes, static, fumes.Wear glasses when clipping leads, because the ends fly. Touch something metal before handling boards. Open a window while soldering, and wash your hands after leaded solder.

Your decisions

  1. 16 MB flash chip: decided 14 Nov, buy two.

    Raw nights (storage option A), plus a spare for other projects. 32–64 MB boards weren't found in India. Adafruit's board (₹594) works with 3.3 V and 5 V boards, so it's the most reusable; the generic JV modules (₹118–148) are fine for this band.

  2. Sensor: SmartElex from Amazon, or Pimoroni from Fab.to.Lab?

    Same chip. The green-LED question applies to both, so the ₹3,000 extra doesn't buy a fix. My lean: SmartElex, then test green at step 4.

  3. Battery: Probots (₹125) or TinyCircuits (₹443)?

    Both 150 mAh with protection. Probots is the better deal; TinyCircuits only makes sense if everything comes from Fab.to.Lab in one order.

  4. Multimeter: one with a µA range.

    Costs about the same as one without, and it's the only way to check the low-power firmware without the ₹10k profiler.

  5. Skin temperature later: TMP117 rather than MAX30205?

    Not needed now. Noted so the build plan doesn't lock in the higher-current part.

  6. Haptic driver: which board?

    SmartElex at Robocraze is ₹152 but was out of stock; SparkFun's is ₹1,595. Same chip, so take whichever is in stock. Either way, buy two or three coin motors.
